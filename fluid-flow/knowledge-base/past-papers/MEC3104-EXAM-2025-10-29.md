## What this paper adds to the folder

**It is the first end-of-semester exam here, and it is a different animal from the four CATs.** Two hours
instead of one; a compulsory 30-mark Question One that alone touches five topic files; and, for the first
time, a *choice* — any two of the remaining four. That choice is worth teaching on its own: Questions Four
and Five are both open-channel-and-weir heavy and share `10-open-channel-flow`, so picking both of them is
the narrowest possible revision bet, while Two and Three are the ones that demand momentum and viscous flow.

**`09-pipe-flow` is examined — but lightly, and that is the news.** Only Q2(a) (choke vs nozzle, §9.6,
3 marks) and Q5(d)(iii) (head loss, §9.2, 3 marks) sit there: **6 of the 90 printed marks**, and both are in
optional questions, so a candidate can legitimately finish this paper having answered no pipe-flow question
at all. Set that against the 2024 CAT 2, which put **14 of 40** into `09`, and against the 2025 CAT 2's 9 of
40. The pattern across five papers now reads: **`09-pipe-flow` is CAT 2 territory, not exam territory.** Tell
him that plainly — it changes how he should split revision time between a CAT 2 and a final.

**`11-drag-and-lift` does not appear at all.** This is the first paper in the folder with no drag, lift,
cylinder, sphere or aerofoil question anywhere — despite `11` being 75 slides and having appeared on all four
CATs. Worth flagging as a live risk in both directions: it was not examined here, but it was examined every
other time.

**Three sections get their first substantial outing.**
- `08-viscous-flow` §8.10 **lubrication and bearings** (Q3(b), 3 marks + Figure 6) — never examined before.
- `05-flow-fundamentals` §5.6 **circulation** (Q1(f), 3 marks) — the 2025 CAT 1 examined vorticity, but not Γ.
- `07-momentum` gets its **second** appearance ever (Q2(c), 6 marks), and it is the same jet pump as 2024.

**And one question is lifted straight out of the deck.** Q1(c) — the weightless triangular prism, faces
labelled $dA$, $dA_1$, $dA_2$ with pressures $p$, $p_1$, $p_2$, asked to show $p = p_1 = p_2$ — is the
derivation at `04-fluid-statics` §4.3, ·slides 84–86, with the slides' own figure. Four marks he can revise
verbatim.

**What the knowledge base does NOT cover: 2 marks, and they are compulsory.** Q1(a) asks him to find the
dimensions of a coefficient by dimensional homogeneity. `_verification-log.md` **P15** already records that
the deck gives the dimension formulas (·slides 41–44) but **never demonstrates the technique** — zero hits
across all 594 slides for *dimensional homogeneity*, *dimensional analysis*, *Buckingham* or *π theorem*.

printed here is negative. See erratum **P33**.
