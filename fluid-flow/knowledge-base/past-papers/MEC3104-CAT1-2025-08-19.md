### Notes that change how a question should be taught

**Q1 — the technique is not in the deck.** Slides 35–44 ("Units and Dimensions") give primary dimensions
{M},{L},{T} (s41) and a table of secondary dimensions with their formulas — Area {L²}, Pressure {ML⁻¹T⁻²},
Energy {ML²T⁻²} (s43–44). Those are the ingredients. But a search of all 594 slides for *dimensional
homogeneity*, *dimensionally consistent*, *dimensional analysis*, *Buckingham*, *Rayleigh* and *π theorem*
returns **zero hits**. The deck never teaches the method of balancing dimensions across an equation to solve for
an unknown coefficient. Teach the method explicitly; do not assume he has seen it.

**Q5 — the paper uses a formulation the deck never gives.** The deck derives the *absolute position*
`y_c = y_G + I_G/(A·y_G)` (s138), measured along the plane from the free surface, and gives
`I_G = bh³/12` (s137) and `P = ρg·sinθ·y_G·A = ρg·h_G·A` (s132). The CAT instead prints
`−I_xx sinθ/(h_cg A)`, which is the *offset from the centroid*. Same physics, different quantity returned.
**This is the root of erratum P5** — the datum ambiguity exists precisely because the paper switched to a form
the deck does not use. Show him both forms side by side.

**Q6(b) — "In a Langrarian flow, one particle is followed along the flow" is not examinable from these notes.**
All 594 slides searched for *Lagrang* and *Euleri*: **zero hits for both.** The deck teaches streamline,
streakline, pathline and stream tube (172–174) and defines steady flow (175), but never introduces the
Lagrangian/Eulerian pair of descriptions. "Control volume" appears (s6, 267, 268, 273) but only in the momentum
context, never contrasted with a particle-following description. The statement as printed happens to be **true**
in standard fluid mechanics — but it tests a term that appears nowhere in his course material.

**Q8 — the deck has almost the same problem.** Slide 189: "SAE 10W oil at 20 °C flows at 1.1 m³/h through a
horizontal pipe with d = 2 cm and L = 12 m. Find (a) the average velocity, (b) the Reynolds number, (c) the
pressure drop, (d) the power required." The CAT changes the oil grade, bore and given velocity. Slide 188 asks
laminar-or-turbulent directly. Worth working slide 189 first as the model.

**Q4 — where each term lives.** Large surface area manometer → s115 (heading: "Measuring differential
(fluctuating) P — the sectional area of one tube is made large"). Inclined manometer → s116 ("Measuring **minute
pressure**"). Metacentric height → s153–154 ("GM is the metacentric height"; "restores the ship's position to
stability"). Relative stationary state → s156, with "equipressure free surface / plane" at s159 and s162.
Compressibility → s65–68 (bulk modulus K, and β = 1/K). Stokes → **ambiguous in the deck**: Stokes' theorem tied
to vorticity at s205, but also Navier–Stokes (27–28, 309) and Stokes flow around a sphere (553). Bernoulli →
Topic 06 (215–263), with head loss at 279 and 393/400.
