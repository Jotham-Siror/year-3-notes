## What this paper adds to the folder

**`09-pipe-flow` stays closed — a second CAT 2 in a row goes there.** The 2024 CAT 2 was the first paper
ever to examine the course's largest section; this one does it again, for **9 of 40 marks**: the bend/elbow
distinction (§9.9, 3 marks), the symbols of Darcy–Weisbach (§9.2, 2 marks) and a numerical head loss
(§9.2–9.3, 4 marks). Add the Reynolds-number and flow-regime parts that feed it and the pipe-flow block is
**13 of 40**. Two papers is a small sample, but on both CAT 2s the pipe-flow question is the spine of the
paper and on neither CAT 1 does it appear at all. If he is revising for a CAT 2, `09` is where the marks are.

**Open-channel flow is the centre of gravity, and it is a bigger showing than any earlier paper.** All
fifteen marks of Question Two sit in `10-open-channel-flow`, against 4 marks on the 2024 CAT 1 and 6 on the
2024 CAT 2. Three sub-topics appear here for the **first time in the folder**: critical depth and minimum
specific energy (§10.6), the subcritical/supercritical distinction (§10.7) and the hydraulic jump (§10.8).

**And the numbers come straight off the slides.** The deck's own worked example at §10.6 (·slides 506–507)
is a **10-ft** channel carrying **180 cfs**; Q2(1)(a) is an **8-ft** channel carrying **150 cfs** — same
problem, nudged numbers, same imperial units, same $g = 32.2$ ft/s². §10.7's example (·slides 510–511)
turns on exactly the subcritical-depth comparison Q2(3) asks him to mark true or false. Revise Topic 10 from
the slides and this question is already done.

**What the knowledge base does NOT cover: 2 marks.** Q2(5) asks him to describe the **"wall effect"** with
an example. A case-insensitive search of all eleven topic files, `_formula-sheet.md` and `_nomenclature.md`
returns **zero hits** for that phrase. The nearest thing in the KB is the *Coandă wall-attachment* note at
slide 432, which `_verification-log.md § Known coverage gaps` already records as **dropped from `09`**. So
the one term on this paper he cannot revise from the KB is also one the KB knows it is missing. Fix that
gap before setting this as a mock.

**Three things recur from the 2024 CAT 2, and one recurs from his own end-of-semester exam.**
- A "with diagrams, distinguish these two pipe fittings, 3 marks" question, for the **third** paper running:
  pipe branch vs junction (2024 CAT 2), bend vs elbow (here), choke vs nozzle (2025 exam Q2(a)).
- A "describe the symbols" instruction attached to a supplied formula — **twice on this paper alone**
  (Q1(3)(a), Q3(2)(i)), and seven times across the five papers now in the folder.
- A flat-plate drag calculation from $D_f = C_D\rho A v^2/2$ (2024 CAT 2 Q3(b) → Q3(2)(ii) here). The 2024
  version misprinted the density as 1200 kg/m³ (erratum P21); this one omits the density altogether (P28).
- **"Define the term: Weir" *(2 marks)*** is printed **word for word** on this paper (Q1(1)) and on the
  end-of-semester exam 22 days later (Q4(a)). Identical wording, identical allocation. That is the strongest
  single revision signal in the folder — see `MEC3104-EXAM-2025-10-29.md`.

**And a warning about a symbol.** This paper writes Darcy–Weisbach with **f**, where the deck, the KB and the
2024 CAT 2 all write **λ**. `_nomenclature.md` reserves `f` for the **Fanning** coefficient, λ/4. Teach him
to read the paper's f as λ and never to mix a KB `f` with a paper `f` — a factor of 4 lives in that gap
(erratum P25).
