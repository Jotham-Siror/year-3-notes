## 1. The four interpretations — sorted on two axes

*For: "Compare the interpretations of the French Revolution."*

| Interpretation | Prime mover | Driven or reactive? | Stance | Associated with |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Machinations of irresponsible intellectuals | ideas, destructively applied | **driven** | hostile | **Edmund Burke** |
| Revolt of respectable middle-class property owners | injustice of the old regime | **reactive** | sympathetic | *(unattributed)* |
| Popular, spontaneous national upheaval | mass poverty; desire for sovereignty | **reactive** | celebratory | **Jules Michelet** |
| Class struggle, nobility versus bourgeoisie | economic class conflict | **driven** | explanatory | **Marxist historians** |

- The axes are the answer. Four positions described is a list; four positions **sorted** is a comparison.
- ·FR p1 for all four.

## 2. The five causes, with the handout's own limits

*For: "Discuss the causes of the French Revolution." — the limits are what turn recitation into argument.*

| # | Cause | Core evidence | The limit the handout attaches |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Intellectuals** | Voltaire, Montesquieu, Rousseau attack church and noble privilege ·FR p1 | They wanted reform, not revolution; no organised movement existed; most participants were illiterate ·FR p1 |
| 2 | **Weak monarchy** | Louis XV governs through flatterers; Louis XVI negligent and badly advised ·FR p2 | *(none stated — the handout treats this as straightforwardly causal)* |
| 3 | **Financial crisis** | Two costly wars; debt intolerable by 1774; inequitable taxation; Turgot dismissed; American war ·FR p2 | The privileged orders, not the crown, blocked the fix — so this is the **aristocratic** revolt ·FR p2 |
| 4 | **Bourgeois–aristocratic conflict** | Estates-General deadlock; 17 June; the Oath; the king's capitulation ·FR p2-3 | *(none stated — presented as the decisive political struggle)* |
| 5 | **Economic crisis and popular revolt** | Bread doubled; Paris unemployment +50%; spring revolts; the Bastille ·FR p4 | **France had suffered depression before 1789 without revolution.** What precipitated intervention was **fear of counter-revolution** ·FR p4 |

## 3. The two phases side by side

## 4. The four revolts of 1787-89

*For: "Who were responsible for the outbreak?" — the sequencing table.*

| Order | Who | Against whom | Wanted | Got |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1st | **the privileged orders** | the crown's tax reform | to keep exemptions ·FR p2 | the Estates-General, and the end of absolutism |
| 2nd | **the bourgeoisie** | the aristocracy | representation and vote by head ·FR p3 | the National Assembly, and a crowd it could not control |
| 3rd | **the urban crowd** | counter-revolution | to defend the Assembly ·FR p4 | the Bastille, and the political initiative ·FR p4 |
| 4th | **the peasantry** | the nobility, and feudal dues | a share of the victory ·FR p5 | land redistribution — and the bourgeoisie's suspicion ·FR p5 |

- The point of the table: **each group's success created the conditions for the next**, and none of them
  intended what followed.

## 8. Method and counter-method

*For: "Account for the defeat of Napoleon." — the table that carries the whole essay.*

| | **Napoleon's method** | **The coalitions' answer** |
|---|---|---|
| Movement | rapid, to strike before the enemy is ready | keep a threat alive, refuse the engagement |
| Aim | prevent coordination; defeat piecemeal | delay until coordination is achieved |
| Supply | live off requisition in the theatre | rely on strong, secure supply lines |
| Requirement | thorough knowledge of the terrain | patience, and space to trade for time |
| Weakness | overextension once the enemy will not stand | slow, and cedes territory meanwhile |
| Where it decided things | Italy 1796; Austerlitz 1805; Jena 1806 | Peninsula from 1810; Russia 1812; Waterloo 1815 |

- All ·RNW ¶11-12. Note the counter-method was proved in Portugal **two years before** Russia — his defeat
  was strategic before it was numerical.

## 9. The Continental System: intent against outcome

| Intended | Actual |
|---|---|
| Blockade Britain indirectly through French-controlled ports ·RNW ¶9 | Britain retaliated; European trade generally seized up ·RNW ¶9 |
| Starve British commerce | Britain **expanded colonial markets** and grew **more prosperous than before** ·RNW ¶9 |
| Bind Europe to France | licences for French merchants read as favouritism and **cost political support** ·RNW ¶9 |
| Enforce compliance | provoked **German nationalism and Romanticism**, and the **Peninsular War** ·NW ¶4 |
| Keep Russia in line | Alexander I **withdrew at the end of 1810** — and the 1812 invasion followed ·NW ¶5 |

## 11. The two reference sources, compared

*For: any question where you must handle your material critically — and for Set E of the quizzes.*

| | **RNW** | **NW** |
|---|---|---|
| Period covered | 1792-1815, one continuous conflict ·¶2 | 1799-1815, the Napoleonic Wars alone ·¶1 |
| Implied view of Napoleon | the Revolution's **heir** | the Revolution's **replacement** |
| Register | narrative, chronological, factual | interpretive, thematic, argumentative |
| Russia 1812 explained by | **Russian strategy** — parallel withdrawal, overextended supply ·¶11 | **the winter** ·¶5 |
| Trafalgar | ended the invasion threat ·¶8 | "just about the only blemish" ·¶3 |
| Waterloo dated | "June 16-18, 1815" — **wrong** (V5) ·¶12 | "June 1815" — vague but not wrong ·¶6 |
| Strongest for | campaigns, treaties, chronology | consequences, legacy, historiographical argument |

- Where they agree, **they are not two witnesses** — both appear to descend from the same standard account.
  Do not present agreement between them as corroboration.
