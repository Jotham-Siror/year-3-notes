# Question bank and essay skeletons — World Civilization II

Built from `knowledge-base/01` and `02`. **Two of these questions are the lecturer's own**, printed in the
handout ·FR p5; they are marked **[SET]**. The rest are derived from the way the notes are organised — where
a handout devotes a numbered section to something, that section is a question waiting to be asked.

**How to use this.** Attempt an outline before reading the skeleton. A skeleton read cold teaches you
nothing; a skeleton read after your own attempt shows you what you left out.

Every skeleton has the same four parts: **the claim** (what you are arguing), **the order** (why the
paragraphs go in that sequence), **the evidence** (with citations, so you can check), and **the trap** (the
mistake this question is designed to catch).

## Q1 [SET] — Discuss the causes of the French Revolution. ·FR p5

**The claim.** No single cause explains it. The Revolution happened because four separate crises — fiscal,
political, social and economic — converged between 1787 and 1789, and because the monarchy was too weak to
manage any of them.

**The order.** Long-term to short-term, structural to immediate. That order is itself an argument: it lets
you end on the trigger rather than the background, which is where the marks are.

**The evidence.**

1. **Intellectual** — Voltaire, Montesquieu, Rousseau attacked church and noble privilege and demanded the
   abolition of feudalism ·FR p1. **Immediately qualify it**: they wanted reform not revolution, there was no
   organised movement, and most of those who acted in 1789 were illiterate and had never heard of them
   ·FR p1. Conclusion: ideas supplied the justification, not the impulse.
2. **Political** — Louis XV left government to flatterers; Louis XVI was negligent and badly advised. The
   result was a breakdown of royal authority and a political vacuum ·FR p2.
3. **Fiscal** — two costly wars (1740-48, 1756-63), a debt intolerable by 1774, an inequitable tax system
   falling on peasants and bourgeoisie, Turgot's reforms defeated by the privileged orders, Necker borrowing
   instead, and the American war (1776-83) making it worse ·FR p2. **This is the aristocratic revolt**: the
   first body to beat the crown was the nobility.
4. **Social** — the Estates-General of 1789, unsummoned since 1614, becomes the arena for the bourgeoisie
   against the aristocracy: the doubling of Third Estate seats, the deadlock over voting by head or by
   order, 17 June, the Tennis Court Oath, the king's capitulation ·FR p2-3.
5. **Economic and popular** — bad harvests, bread doubled, Paris unemployment up 50%, spontaneous revolts in
   spring 1789 ·FR p4.

**The conclusion.** Use the handout's own qualification, because it is the best sentence in your notes:
economic hardship alone had not produced revolution before 1789. What precipitated popular intervention was
**fear of counter-revolution** — royal troops around Paris and Versailles, rumours of foreign intervention
·FR p4. Hunger explains the crowd; fear explains the timing.

**The trap.** Writing five paragraphs that each say "this caused the Revolution" with no weighting and no
interaction. The handout attaches limits to every cause; an essay that does not is reciting, not arguing.

## Q2 [SET] — Who were responsible for the outbreak of the French Revolution? ·FR p5

**The claim.** This is not Q1 reworded. Q1 asks *what*; this asks *who*, which means assigning agency — and
the honest answer is that the people who began the Revolution were not the people who intended one.

**The order.** By the order in which each group acted.

**The evidence.**

1. **The privileged orders** acted first and are the most under-credited answer. They blocked Turgot's
   removal of aristocratic tax exemptions and forced his dismissal; they blocked the later proposals and
   paralysed government ·FR p2. Without their obstruction there is no fiscal crisis and no Estates-General.
2. **The monarchy** — through weakness rather than action. Louis XVI capitulated to aristocratic pressure,
   then convened the Estates-General as a last resort, which the handout calls **an abdication of royal power
   and an invitation to revolution** ·FR p2. Quote that phrase.
3. **The bourgeoisie** — the first group to act *deliberately* towards revolution: the self-constitution as
   the National Assembly on 17 June, the Oath, the refusal to disperse ·FR p3.
4. **The urban crowd and the peasantry** — the Bastille, the municipal revolutions, the Great Fear ·FR p4-5.
   Note they acted defensively, to forestall counter-revolution.
5. **The intellectuals** — responsible for the language, not the act ·FR p1.

**The conclusion.** Responsibility is sequential, not shared: each group's action created the conditions for
the next. The nobility intended to defend privilege and destroyed the monarchy; the bourgeoisie intended a
constitution and released the crowd; the crowd intended to defend the Assembly and took the initiative from
it ·FR p4.

**The trap.** Answering Q1 again. If your essay does not name groups and rank them, you have not answered
this question.

## Q3 — Account for the radicalization of the Revolution, 1791-1793.

**The claim.** Radicalization was not a change of mind but a response to threat: the moderate settlement was
attacked from outside and inside simultaneously, and war destroyed it.

**The order.** The handout's own five, but presented as a chain rather than a list — that reordering is the
value you add.

**The evidence.**

- **Fear**: (a) counter-revolution — émigré nobles raising troops abroad, the Church opposing at home,
  supporters demanding repression of enemies "real or imaginary" ·FR p7; and (b) royalist plots — Louis XVI
  soliciting Austria, Prussia, Sweden and Spain 1789-91, then the flight of June 1791 ·FR p7-8.
- **Grievance**: (c) economic crisis unabated, capital flight, the land settlement freezing agrarian
  capitalism, extremists blaming the Revolution's enemies for the paralysis ·FR p8.
- **Vehicle**: (d) division in the revolutionary camp — moderates content with constitutional monarchy,
  radicals wanting manhood suffrage and redistribution, and a foreign-policy split between exporting the
  Revolution and fearing military dictatorship ·FR p8.
- **Detonator**: (e) war, April 1792 ·FR p9.

**The conclusion.** The immediate victim was the monarchy; the war "inaugurated the reign of terror" ·FR p9.
And the politicians who warned that war would produce a **military dictatorship** were right — that is where
`02` begins.

**The trap.** The date **V1**: France declared war on **Austria alone** on 20 April 1792, not on Prussia and
Austria. Your handout has this wrong. Prussia joined afterwards.

## Q4 — "The French Revolution was a bourgeois revolution." Discuss.

**The claim.** True of the summer of 1789 and of the settlement of 1789-91; inadequate for everything else.

**The order.** Concede, then complicate, then resolve.

**The evidence.**

- **For.** The handout itself calls the king's capitulation "the political triumph of the bourgeoisie in what
  had been called the bourgeois revolution" ·FR p3. The settlement is bourgeois in content: careers open to
  talent, property protected, feudal restriction removed, an internal free market with protective external
  tariffs ·FR p6-7. The Declaration is silent on popular democracy and freedom of association ·FR p6.
- **Against.** The first successful revolt was **aristocratic**, not bourgeois ·FR p2. The decisive
  intervention of July 1789 was **popular**, and after it "political initiative passed from the National
  Assembly to the popular forces of revolution" ·FR p4. The peasant revolution was **distinct and autonomous**
  ·FR p4, and the peasants demanded a share the bourgeoisie were reluctant to grant ·FR p5.
- **The historiography.** Interpretation 4 (Marxist) makes it class struggle; interpretation 3 (Michelet)
  makes it national and spontaneous; interpretation 2 makes it a defensive revolt of property ·FR p1. Name
  them.

**The conclusion.** Quote the handout's own verdict: the peasants were **"both allies and potential enemies
of the bourgeois revolution"** ·FR p5. A revolution that needed allies it feared is not simply bourgeois.

**The trap.** Treating "bourgeois revolution" as a fact rather than one of four contested interpretations.

## Q5 — Assess the achievements of the moderate phase, 1789-1791.

**The claim.** It transformed France's institutions permanently but built a settlement that could not defend
itself.

**The evidence — what it achieved** ·FR p5-7: the Declaration of Rights and its eight provisions; a
constitutional monarchy with sovereignty in the nation; an independent judiciary with a high court and court
of appeal; uniform administration replacing the old structure; feudalism, noble privilege and titles
abolished; clerical privilege abolished and freedom of worship for Protestants and Jews; church and noble
land redistributed to the peasants; an internal free market with external tariffs; equitable taxation, tithes
and the salt tax abolished; government bonds circulating as notes.

**What it failed to do.** The Declaration was silent on economic freedom, popular democracy, freedom of
association, and slavery ·FR p6. The religious settlement produced a bitter state-church conflict ·FR p6 that
put the Church on the counter-revolutionary side ·FR p7. And the settlement satisfied neither the radicals
nor the king ·FR p8.

**The conclusion.** By the end of 1791 "the revolution appeared to be over" ·FR p7 — and within a year the
monarchy was gone. The phase is best assessed as durable in its institutions and fatal in its politics.

**The trap.** Listing the reforms without assessing them. The question says *assess*.

## Q6 — Why did the Continental System fail?

**The claim.** It failed because it was a blockade imposed by a land power against a sea power, and its costs
fell on the countries enforcing it.

**The evidence** ·RNW ¶9, ·NW ¶4:

1. **Britain retaliated in kind**, detaining ships bound for French ports, so European trade generally seized
   up.
2. **French licences** allowing French merchants to trade with Britain anyway looked like favouritism and
   **cost Napoleon considerable political support**.
3. **Britain expanded colonial markets** and emerged **more prosperous than before**.
4. **It generated two revolts**: German nationalism and Romanticism as a reaction against French rule; and in
   Spain the **Peninsular War**, a guerrilla war that **diverted French forces from the rest of Europe**.
5. **It lost him Russia.** Alexander I withdrew at the end of 1810 ·NW ¶5 — and the invasion of 1812 followed
   directly.

**The conclusion.** Trace the chain to its end: the System produced the Peninsular War and the Russian
campaign, which are the two theatres that destroyed the Grand Army. The blockade did not merely fail; it
supplied the means of Napoleon's defeat.

**The trap.** Stopping at "it hurt Europe more than Britain". That is the first half. The examinable half is
the causal link to 1808 and 1812.

## Q7 — Account for the defeat of Napoleon.

**The claim.** His method was understood and answered. The coalitions stopped fighting the war he was good at.

**The order.** Method → counter-method → the three theatres where it worked → the arithmetic.

**The evidence** ·RNW ¶11-12:

- **His method**: rapid movement, surprise, preventing coordination, defeating enemies piecemeal — requiring
  terrain knowledge and living off requisition.
- **The answer**: maintain a threat, avoid engagement until coordinated, rely on strong supply lines, and let
  his own lines overextend.
- **Peninsula** — Wellington, supplied through Portugal.
- **Russia 1812** — Barclay de Tolly and Bagration withdrawing along parallel lines; Borodino (7 September)
  indecisive; the retreat along a route stripped of forage; **500,000 lost**, plus his allies' faith and his
  enemies' awe.
- **Waterloo, 18 June 1815** — the same failure: he could not surprise, and could not prevent Wellington and
  Blücher joining.
- **Arithmetic**: by 1813 the coalition armies **at last outnumbered** France's, and his allies fell away.

**The conclusion.** Note that his defeat was strategic before it was numerical — the method was answered in
Portugal in 1810, two years before Russia.

**The trap.** Two dates. **V5**: Waterloo was **18 June 1815**; one of your sources says "June 16-18", which
is the campaign, and 16 June is Ligny and Quatre Bras. **D2**: do not explain 1812 by "the Russian winter" —
that is the weaker of your two sources' explanations; the winter finished an army that Russian strategy had
already defeated.

## Q8 — To what extent did Napoleon continue the work of the French Revolution?

**The claim.** He universalised its institutions while destroying its politics.

**The evidence — continuation** ·NW ¶2, ¶8, ¶10: the **Napoleonic Code**, imposed on occupied countries and
still the basis of much Continental law; **careers open to talent**, which made France more efficient,
powerful and patriotic; the spread of legal equality so effective that **his opponents adopted it** — Stein
and Hardenberg rebuilding Prussia on French lines. Napoleon himself, "a minor Corsican aristocrat who rose to
be Emperor", as the embodiment of the social mobility the Revolution created ·NW ¶8.

**The evidence — betrayal**: war aims reverted to **simple aggrandizement of influence and territory**
·RNW ¶2; he **declared himself Emperor** in 1804 ·NW ¶2; and the politicians of 1791-92 who feared war would
produce a **military dictatorship** ·FR p8 were describing him in advance.

**The conclusion.** The strongest version: he was the Revolution's executor abroad and its undertaker at
home. The Code travelled; the Republic did not.

**The trap.** The tension inside your own source. `NW ¶12` says the wars were driven on all sides by
**greed**, and `NW ¶10` says Napoleon spread liberal reform everywhere he went. Both cannot be the whole
truth. Pointing that out is a first-class move; ignoring it and quoting both is not.

## Q9 — Assess the significance of the Congress of Vienna.

**The claim.** It bought stability by freezing politics, and both halves of that trade were deliberate.

**The evidence** ·NW ¶7, ¶14: the five negotiators — Metternich, Castlereagh, Alexander I, Hardenberg,
Talleyrand; a balance in which **no one power could dominate** as France had; an order **balanced enough that
future rising powers could be stopped by coalitions**; and, on the other side, the **protection of
conservative regimes** against the liberal change Napoleon had spread, setting up the conflict between
liberalism and conservatism **from 1815 to 1848**.

**The conclusion.** Vienna succeeded on its own terms and failed on the Revolution's. It is the reason there
was no general European war for ninety-nine years, and the reason there were revolutions in 1830 and 1848.

**The trap.** **V7**: do not repeat the claim that **no** Europe-wide conflict occurred between 1815 and
1914. The Crimean War (1853-56) did, as did the unification wars. Say **no general war involving all the
great powers** — the accurate claim is still impressive.

## Q10 — Compare the interpretations of the French Revolution.

**The claim.** The four readings disagree less about facts than about what kind of thing a revolution is.

**The evidence** ·FR p1: **Burke** — machinations of irresponsible intellectuals against time-honoured
institutions; **the second reading** — a revolt of respectable middle-class property owners against
injustice; **Michelet** — a popular, spontaneous upheaval of the whole nation against mass poverty, for
national sovereignty and popular democracy; **the Marxists** — class struggle between a landed nobility
defending the old order and a bourgeoisie clearing the way for modern capitalism.

**The structure that lifts this answer.** Sort them on two axes. **Caused or reactive**: Burke and the
Marxists make the Revolution driven (by ideas, by class); readings 2 and 3 make it a response to injustice.
**Approving or hostile**: Burke condemns, Michelet celebrates, the Marxists explain.

**The conclusion.** Each belongs to its moment — Burke a hostile contemporary of 1790, Michelet a
nineteenth-century republican, the Marxists a twentieth-century school. Then say which the evidence in your
notes supports best, and why.

**The trap.** Describing four positions and stopping. A comparison question wants the axes.
