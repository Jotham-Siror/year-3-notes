# Flashcards — World Civilization II

108 cards in 11 decks, built from `knowledge-base/01`, `02` and `_chronology.md`. Every card carries its
citation on the back, so a card you doubt can be checked against the source in one step.

**`flashcards.csv` sits beside this file** — three columns (Front, Back, Deck), ready to import into Anki or
any flashcard app. The two files are generated together and always agree.

**Deck 11 is the one to drill hardest.** Those seven cards are the places where your own source material is
wrong; each names its log entry so you can read the full correction in `knowledge-base/_verification-log.md`.

**A.** Jules Michelet (interpretation 3) ·FR p1

**Q.** Which interpretation makes the Revolution a revolt of respectable middle-class property owners?

**A.** Interpretation 2, unattributed in the handout ·FR p1

**Q.** Which event is the Revolution said to be comparable to in European life?

**A.** The Protestant Reformation of the sixteenth century ·FR p1

**Q.** Which five ideas does the handout say the Revolution released beyond France?

## 02 Causes  *(20 cards)*

**Q.** Name the three philosophes the handout cites.

**A.** Voltaire, Montesquieu, Rousseau ·FR p1

---

**Q.** Give the three limits on the intellectuals' responsibility.

**A.** They wanted reform not revolution; there was no organised movement to turn their ideas into a programme; most participants in 1789 were illiterate and had not heard of them ·FR p1

---

**Q.** Reign dates of Louis XV and Louis XVI?

**A.** Louis XV 1715-1774; Louis XVI 1774-1793 ·FR p2

---

**Q.** What was Louis XV more interested in than governing?

**A.** Food and women - he left decisions to courtiers who excelled only in flattery and intrigue ·FR p2

---

**Q.** Which two wars drained French finances, and when?

**A.** War of the Austrian Succession 1740-48; Seven Years War 1756-63 ·FR p2

---

**Q.** By what year had the national debt become an intolerable burden?

**A.** 1774 ·FR p2

---

**Q.** Who bore the heaviest tax burden under the old regime?

**A.** The peasants and the bourgeoisie - the unprivileged orders ·FR p2 (printed 'persons', C8)

---

**Q.** Who was Turgot and what happened to him?

**A.** Louis XVI's finance minister; proposed removing aristocratic tax exemptions; the nobility and church opposed and the king dismissed him ·FR p2

---

**Q.** What did Necker do instead of reforming?

**A.** Resorted to more public borrowing to appease the aristocracy ·FR p2

---

**Q.** Which foreign intervention deepened the fiscal crisis, and when?

**A.** The American War of Independence, 1776-1783 ·FR p2

---

**Q.** What is meant by the aristocratic revolt?

**A.** The privileged orders' defeat of royal tax reform - the first body to beat the crown was the nobility, not the Third Estate ·FR p2

---

**Q.** When had the Estates-General last met before 1789?

**A.** 1614 ·FR p2

---

**Q.** What phrase does the handout use for the decision to convene the Estates-General?

**A.** An abdication of royal power and an invitation to revolution ·FR p2

---

**Q.** What were the three orders?

**A.** Clergy (First Estate), Aristocracy (Second Estate), Third Estate or commoners ·FR p2

---

**Q.** How were the seats allocated in 1789?

**A.** 600 to the Third Estate, 300 each to clergy and nobility ·FR p3

---

**Q.** Why was doubling the Third Estate's seats worthless alone?

**A.** Because voting by order would still let the other two outvote it 2-1; the concessions only work together ·FR p3

---

**Q.** What happened to the price of bread and to Paris unemployment before 1789?

**A.** Bread doubled; Paris unemployment rose by 50% ·FR p4

---

**Q.** What, rather than economic crisis, precipitated popular intervention?

**A.** Fear of counter-revolution - royal troops around Paris and Versailles, and rumours of foreign intervention ·FR p4

---

**Q.** What three things did peasants fear in the Great Fear?

**A.** Aristocratic conspiracy, foreign invasion, and the intentions of the bourgeoisie ·FR p5

---

**Q.** What does the handout conclude about the peasants' role?

**A.** They were both allies and potential enemies of the bourgeois revolution ·FR p5

## 03 1789  *(7 cards)*

**Q.** What happened on 17 June 1789?

**A.** The Third Estate constituted itself the National Assembly and invited the other orders to join ·FR p3

---

**Q.** When did the majority of the clergy vote to join?

**A.** 19 June 1789, two days later (V4) ·FR p3

---

**Q.** Date and name of the Tennis Court Oath?

**A.** 20 June 1789 - the Serment du Jeu de Paume (V2; the handout calls it a 'resolution' and misplaces it) ·FR p3

---

**Q.** What did Louis XVI do on 23 June 1789?

**A.** Summoned the Estates-General, promised a constitutional monarchy, ordered the orders to meet separately, and ordered the National Assembly dissolved - and was defied ·FR p3

---

**Q.** When was the National Assembly renamed, and to what?

**A.** July 1789, to the Constituent Assembly ·FR p3

---

**Q.** Date and significance of the storming of the Bastille?

**A.** 14 July 1789 - symbolised the fall of royal despotism; followed by civilian militias and municipal governments across France ·FR p4

---

**Q.** What shifted after 14 July 1789?

**A.** Political initiative passed from the National Assembly to the popular forces of the Revolution ·FR p4

## 04 Moderate phase  *(11 cards)*

**Q.** When was the Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen issued?

**A.** August 1789 ·FR p5

---

**Q.** Which two influences shaped the reforms of 1789-91?

**A.** The French intellectuals' liberal and democratic ideas, and the American Bill of Rights ·FR p5

---

**Q.** Name four rights in the Declaration.

**A.** Equality before the law; freedom of speech and the press; freedom of conscience; freedom from arbitrary arrest; equitable taxation; the right of rebellion; office by talent not birth; private property ·FR p5-6

---

**Q.** On what four subjects was the Declaration silent?

**A.** Economic enterprise and trade; popular democracy; freedom of association; slavery and the slave trade ·FR p6

---

**Q.** Where did sovereignty rest under the new constitution?

**A.** In the nation - that is, in the National Parliament of elected representatives ·FR p6

---

**Q.** What three powers did the king retain?

**A.** Appointing ministers, ambassadors and commanders; declaring war or making peace with parliamentary consent; suspending or delaying non-financial legislation for up to four years ·FR p6

---

**Q.** Which religious minorities gained freedom of worship?

**A.** Protestants and Jews ·FR p6

---

**Q.** What was done with Church and noble lands?

**A.** Divided and redistributed to the peasants ·FR p6

---

**Q.** Describe the economic settlement in one line.

**A.** Internal free market - tolls and customs posts removed, feudal restrictions swept away - with external tariffs retained to protect national industry ·FR p6-7

---

**Q.** Which two old taxes were abolished?

**A.** Church tithes and the salt tax ·FR p7

---

**Q.** What happened to the government bonds introduced to raise revenue?

**A.** They were soon used as bank notes - these are the assignats, which the handout never names ·FR p7

**Q.** Which four powers did Louis XVI covertly solicit, 1789-91?

**A.** June 1791 - the Flight to Varennes; he was arrested and returned to Paris a prisoner, and became more unpopular than ever ·FR p8

**Q.** What did the radicals demand that the moderates did not?

**A.** Universal manhood suffrage and redistribution of wealth to produce economic equality ·FR p8

**Q.** What did the anti-war politicians of 1791-92 predict?

**Q.** When was the First French Republic proclaimed?

**A.** 1792 ·FR p8

**Q.** What was the Declaration of Pillnitz?

## 06 The wars  *(9 cards)*

**Q.** What years do the Revolutionary and Napoleonic Wars span?

**A.** 1792-1815 in one source; the Napoleonic Wars alone dated 1799-1815 in the other (D1) ·RNW ¶2, ·NW ¶1

---

**Q.** When do the revolutionary wars conveniently end, and what changed?

**A.** By 1801; with Napoleon's rise to absolute power, war aims reverted to simple aggrandizement of influence and territory ·RNW ¶2

---

**Q.** How many coalitions opposed France?

**A.** Seven, over 23 years from 1793 ·RNW ¶4

---

**Q.** Compare army sizes before and after 1800.

**A.** Mid-eighteenth century 60,000-70,000; Napoleon after 1800 routinely 250,000; Russia 1812 some 600,000 ·RNW ¶4

---

**Q.** What was the levée en masse?

**A.** The decree placing all Frenchmen at the army's disposal, producing armies of unprecedented size ·RNW ¶4

---

**Q.** Who commanded at Valmy, when, and against whom?

**A.** Dumouriez and Kellermann, 20 September 1792, against a Prussian-Austrian force ·RNW ¶4

---

**Q.** Name the First Coalition's members.

**A.** Austria, Prussia, Spain, the United Provinces, Great Britain - early 1793 ·RNW ¶4

---

**Q.** What did Prussia do in 1795?

**A.** Left the coalition and signed a separate peace that held until 1806 ·RNW ¶6

---

**Q.** What became the Batavian Republic, and when?

**A.** The United Provinces of the Netherlands, May 1795 ·RNW ¶6

## 08 Continental System  *(4 cards)*

**Q.** What was the Berlin Decree, and when?

**A.** 1806 - ships calling at British ports before reaching French-controlled ports became liable to seizure; it created the Continental System ·RNW ¶9

---

**Q.** Give four reasons the Continental System failed.

**A.** British retaliation froze European trade; licences to French merchants looked like favouritism and cost political support; Britain expanded colonial markets and grew richer; it provoked German nationalism and the Spanish Peninsular War ·RNW ¶9, ·NW ¶4

---

**Q.** What did enforcing the System produce in Spain?

**A.** The Peninsular War - a protracted guerrilla war that diverted French forces from the rest of Europe ·NW ¶4

---

**Q.** What were the Spanish guerrillas fighting for?

**A.** To return a Bourbon to the throne - a conservative aim, while German opposition wanted more self-rule ·NW ¶11

## 09 Defeat  *(14 cards)*

**Q.** Describe Napoleon's military method.

**A.** Move armies rapidly, strike quickly and often by surprise, prevent his enemies coordinating, and defeat them piecemeal - requiring terrain knowledge and living off requisition ·RNW ¶11

---

**Q.** What was the answer his enemies found?

**A.** Maintain a threat while avoiding engagement until coordinated, rely on strong supply lines, and let his own overextend ·RNW ¶11

---

**Q.** Who used that strategy first, and supplied from where?

**A.** Wellington in the Peninsula, supplied through Portugal ·RNW ¶11

---

**Q.** Which Russian generals answered the 1812 invasion, and how?

**A.** Barclay de Tolly and Bagration - they simply withdrew along parallel lines ·RNW ¶11

---

**Q.** What triggered the invasion of Russia?

**A.** Alexander I withdrew Russia from the Continental System at the end of 1810 ·NW ¶5

---

**Q.** Borodino - date and result?

**A.** 7 September 1812; the only full-scale engagement of the campaign, and indecisive, so Napoleon was eventually forced to retreat ·RNW ¶11

---

**Q.** What did Napoleon lose in Russia?

**A.** 500,000 men, the faith of his allies, and the awe of his enemies ·RNW ¶11

---

**Q.** Leipzig - when and what?

**A.** October 1813 - defeat by an international coalition of armies ·NW ¶5

---

**Q.** When did Napoleon first abdicate, and where was he exiled?

**A.** 6 April 1814; exiled to Elba, which lasted less than a year ·RNW ¶12

---

**Q.** What were the Hundred Days?

**A.** March to June 1815 - Napoleon's return from Elba, the army he raised, and the brief final campaign ·RNW ¶12, ·NW ¶6

---

**Q.** Name the Seventh Coalition.

**A.** Great Britain, Russia, Prussia, Austria - 1815 ·RNW ¶12

---

**Q.** Date of Waterloo, and what happened two days before?

**A.** 18 June 1815; on 16 June, Ligny and Quatre Bras - different battles, different places (V5) ·RNW ¶12

---

**Q.** Why did Napoleon lose at Waterloo, in the source's analysis?

**A.** The same failure as in Russia - he could not surprise, and could not prevent two armies invading along separate lines from joining ·RNW ¶12

---

**Q.** Where did Napoleon die?

**A.** Saint Helena, in the South Atlantic ·NW ¶6

## 10 Vienna and legacy  *(9 cards)*

**Q.** Name the five Vienna negotiators and their powers.

**A.** Metternich (Austria), Castlereagh (Britain), Alexander I (Russia), Hardenberg (Prussia), Talleyrand (France) ·NW ¶7

---

**Q.** What did the Congress achieve, and what did it protect?

**A.** A balance in which no one power could dominate and rising powers could be stopped by coalitions - and it protected conservative regimes against liberal change ·NW ¶7, ¶14

---

**Q.** What conflict did the Congress set the stage for?

**A.** Liberalism versus conservatism, 1815 to the revolutionary year 1848 ·NW ¶14

---

**Q.** What survived of Napoleon's rule in law?

**A.** The Napoleonic Code, imposed on occupied countries, forming the legal basis for much of Continental European law today ·NW ¶10

---

**Q.** Which Prussian reformers rebuilt their state on French lines, and why?

**A.** Baron Stein and Hardenberg - because to compete with France they had to resemble it ·NW ¶10

---

**Q.** Which intellectual movement did Germany champion against French rationalism?

**A.** Romanticism ·NW ¶4, ¶11

---

**Q.** Why, according to the source, did Britain win the wars?

**A.** It emerged in 1815 with a commercial powerhouse economy, the pre-eminent navy and a large colonial network - and national unity against Napoleon carried it through a dangerous phase of its Industrial Revolution ·NW ¶13

---

**Q.** Which two New World events echoed the wars?

**A.** The War of 1812 and Toussaint l'Ouverture's regime in Haiti ·NW ¶9

---

**Q.** What is the source's moral verdict on the period?

**A.** That right and wrong are hard to distinguish - Napoleon a dictator but not a particularly evil one, and all sides driven by greed in a period typified by Realism. Treat as interpretation, not fact ·NW ¶12

## 11 Traps  *(7 cards)*

**Q.** TRAP: against whom did France declare war in April 1792?

**A.** Austria alone, 20 April 1792. The handout says 'Prussia and Austria'. Prussia joined afterwards. (V1)

---

**Q.** TRAP: when was the Tennis Court Oath, relative to the royal session?

**A.** 20 June 1789 - three days BEFORE the royal session of 23 June, not a response to it. And it is an Oath, not a 'resolution'. (V2)

---

**Q.** TRAP: was eighteenth-century French foreign policy successful?

**A.** No. The handout says 'costly but successful' in the same sentence as two lost wars. Read costly and unsuccessful - and note the Austrian Succession was fruitless rather than lost. (V3)

---

**Q.** TRAP: how long after 17 June did the clergy join?

**A.** Two days - 19 June 1789. The handout prints the unreadable 'This day later'. (V4)

---

**Q.** TRAP: date of the Battle of Waterloo?

**A.** 18 June 1815. 'June 16-18' describes the campaign; 16 June is Ligny and Quatre Bras. (V5)

---

**Q.** TRAP: where was the Parthenopean Republic?

**A.** Naples. Piedmont's was the Subalpine Republic. Parthenope is the old Greek name for Naples. (V6)

---

**Q.** TRAP: was there a Europe-wide war between 1815 and 1914?

**A.** Yes - the Crimean War 1853-56, and the unification wars. The defensible claim is that no GENERAL war involving all the great powers recurred until 1914. (V7)
