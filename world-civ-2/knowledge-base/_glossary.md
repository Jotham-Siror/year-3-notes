# Glossary — World Civilization II

Terms as the sources use them. **Read the clash table first** — it is the section that costs marks when it is
skipped. `[added]` marks a term the sources rely on but never name.

## § The clash table — things that are easily confused

These are the pairs that look alike, sound alike, or share a name, and that a marker will notice.

| # | The confusion | The distinction |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **National Assembly** vs **Constituent Assembly** | **One body, two names.** Constituted 17 June 1789 as the National Assembly, **renamed the Constituent Assembly in July 1789** ·FR p3. Do not treat them as successive bodies. |
| 2 | **Estates-General** vs **National Assembly** | **Two different bodies, three days apart.** The Estates-General was the king's summoned body of three orders (last met 1614). The National Assembly was the Third Estate's self-constitution *out of* it on 17 June. The first was called; the second seized. |
| 3 | **The Great Fear** vs **the Terror** | **Four years apart, opposite directions.** The Great Fear is the rural panic of **July-August 1789** — peasants fearing aristocratic conspiracy, invasion, *and* the bourgeoisie ·FR p5. The Terror is the state violence of **1793-94**, named once in your handout's last line ·FR p9 and never explained. |
| 4 | **Hardenberg** appears twice | **The same man, two roles.** Prussian reformer rebuilding the state on French lines ·NW ¶10, **and** Prussia's negotiator at Vienna ·NW ¶7. |
| 5 | **Alexander I** | Russia's ruler throughout — Napoleon's **enemy** (Third Coalition), then **ally** (Tilsit 1807), then enemy again (1810-12), then a **negotiator at Vienna** ·NW ¶3, ¶5, ¶7. Three positions, one man. |
| 6 | **Berlin Decree** vs **Continental System** | **Instrument vs policy.** The Berlin Decree (1806) is the legal act; the Continental System is the blockade policy it created ·RNW ¶9. Not synonyms. |
| 7 | **Louis XVI** vs **Louis XVIII** | The Bourbon restored in 1814 is **Louis XVIII**, not the executed king's immediate successor by number — **no Louis XVII ever reigned** `[added]`. |
| 8 | **Jena** and **Auerstädt** | **Two battles fought the same day in 1806**, often written as one hyphenated name ·RNW ¶8, ·NW ¶3. Your sources list them separately and together; either is acceptable, but know they are two. |
| 9 | **Waterloo** vs **Ligny / Quatre Bras** | **18 June** vs **16 June 1815**. Different battles, different places. One source blurs them into "June 16-18" — see **V5**. |
| 10 | **Parthenopean** vs **Subalpine** Republic | **Naples** vs **Piedmont**. One source puts the Parthenopean in Piedmont — see **V6**. Parthenope is the old Greek name for Naples: that is the check. |
| 11 | **Revolutionary Wars** vs **Napoleonic Wars** | The sources disagree on where the line falls — 1792-1801 then 1799-1815, with an overlap. See **D1**. State which framing you are using. |
| 12 | **Bourgeoisie** vs **Third Estate** | Not identical. The **Third Estate** is a legal order containing everyone who is neither clergy nor noble — peasants included. The **bourgeoisie** is the propertied middle class that led it. The handout's whole §1.2.5 turns on the two coming apart ·FR p5. |

## § Institutions and bodies

- **Old regime** — the pre-1789 order of absolute monarchy, aristocratic privilege, clerical privilege and
  feudal obligation ·FR p1-2.
- **Estates-General** — the representative body of the three orders: **Clergy** (First Estate), **Aristocracy**
  (Second Estate), **Third Estate** or commoners. Not summoned between **1614 and 1789** ·FR p2.
- **Third Estate** — the commoners; granted **600 seats to the other orders' 300 each** in 1789, having argued
  that as the majority of the nation it deserved more representation ·FR p3.
- **National Assembly / Constituent Assembly** — see clash 1 ·FR p3.
- **National Parliament** — the elected legislature in which **sovereignty rested** under the constitution of
  the moderate phase ·FR p6.
- **The Directory** — the government Napoleon overthrew in 1799 ·NW ¶2. **Never explained in any source you
  hold.**
- **The Consulate** — the regime established at Brumaire, with Napoleon as **First Consul** ·RNW ¶8, ·NW ¶2.
- **Congress of Vienna** — the 1814-15 conference that reorganised Europe; negotiators **Metternich**
  (Austria), **Castlereagh** (Britain), **Alexander I** (Russia), **Hardenberg** (Prussia), **Talleyrand**
  (France) ·NW ¶7.

## § Events and episodes

- **Aristocratic revolt** — the privileged orders' defeat of royal tax reform, 1774-88; the first successful
  resistance to the crown ·FR p2.
- **Tennis Court Oath** — 20 June 1789; the deputies' undertaking not to separate until a constitution was
  drawn up. The handout calls it a "resolution" and misplaces it — **V2** ·FR p3.
- **Storming of the Bastille** — 14 July 1789; symbolised the fall of royal despotism ·FR p4.
- **The Great Fear** — see clash 3 ·FR p5.
- **Flight to Varennes** `[added name]` — Louis XVI's failed escape of June 1791; he was arrested and
  returned to Paris a prisoner ·FR p8.
- **Declaration of Pillnitz** — August 1791; Austria and Prussia's call to European rulers to restore the
  French king ·RNW ¶4.
- **Levée en masse** — the decree placing all Frenchmen at the army's disposal, which produced armies of
  unprecedented size ·RNW ¶4. Printed "levy en masse" — **C21**.
- **18 Brumaire** — the coup of 9 November 1799 ·RNW ¶8.
- **The Hundred Days** — March to June 1815, from Napoleon's return from Elba to Waterloo ·RNW ¶12, ·NW ¶6.
- **Thermidorean Reaction** — named once as the phase into which the Revolution "quieted" before 1799
  ·NW ¶2. **Not explained.**

## § Documents, laws and policies

- **Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen** — August 1789; the eight rights the handout lists,
  and the four silences it notes ·FR p5-6.
- **The constitution of the moderate phase** — made France a constitutional monarchy with sovereignty in the
  nation; the king kept appointment, war-and-peace and **suspensive veto** powers, the last for up to four
  years ·FR p6.
- **Assignats** `[added name]` — the handout's "government bonds introduced to raise revenue [which] were soon
  used as bank notes" ·FR p7. It never names them.
- **Concordat of 1801** — Napoleon's normalisation of relations with the Church ·NW ¶2.
- **Napoleonic Code** — the streamlined French law system; imposed on occupied countries and now the **legal
  basis for much of Continental European law** ·NW ¶2, ¶10.
- **Continental System / Berlin Decree** — see clash 6 ·RNW ¶9, ·NW ¶4.

## § People

- **Voltaire, Montesquieu, Rousseau** — the intellectuals whose critique supplied the Revolution's
  ideological justification, though not its organisation ·FR p1.
- **Edmund Burke** — interpretation 1: the Revolution as the work of irresponsible intellectuals ·FR p1.
- **Jules Michelet** — interpretation 3: a spontaneous upheaval of the whole nation ·FR p1.
- **Turgot** — Louis XVI's reformist finance minister, dismissed under aristocratic pressure ·FR p2.
- **Necker** — his successor, who borrowed instead of reforming ·FR p2.
- **Dumouriez and Kellermann** — the French commanders at Valmy, 1792 ·RNW ¶4.
- **Nelson** — victor at the Nile (1798) and Trafalgar (1805) ·RNW ¶8.
- **Wellington** — the Peninsular commander, and joint victor at Waterloo ·RNW ¶11-12, ·NW ¶6.
- **Blücher** — the Prussian commander at Waterloo ·RNW ¶12, ·NW ¶6.
- **Barclay de Tolly and Bagration** — the Russian generals whose parallel withdrawal defeated the 1812
  invasion ·RNW ¶11.
- **Baron Stein and Hardenberg** — Prussian reformers who rebuilt their state on French lines ·NW ¶10.
- **Marie Louise of Austria** — Napoleon's second wife from 1810; mother of the **King of Rome** ·NW ¶5.
- **Toussaint l'Ouverture** — his regime in Haiti named as one of the wars' New World echoes ·NW ¶9.

## § Concepts and -isms

- **Liberalism, socialism, democracy, fraternity, nationalism** — the ideas the handout says the Revolution
  released beyond France's frontiers ·FR p1.
- **Romanticism** — the German intellectual rebellion against the French Enlightenment rationalism Napoleon
  carried ·NW ¶4, ¶11.
- **Nationalism** — mobilised *against* Napoleon, most notably in Germany, by previously disunited
  linguistic-ethnic groups reacting to French rule ·NW ¶11.
- **"Realism" in diplomacy** — the source's characterisation of the period: all sides pursuing advantage
  rather than defending values ·NW ¶12. An interpretation, not a fact.
- **Counter-revolution** — the émigré nobility's and the Church's project to restore the old regime, backed
  from abroad ·FR p7.
