## Question One · 4 marks · → `03-binomial-theorem` §4, §5

> [q] Expand $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-2t}}$ in ascending powers of $t$ as far as the term in $t^3$. State the
> limits of $t$ for which the expression is valid. *[4 Marks]*

```yaml
given:
  expression: "1/√(1−2t)"
  as_a_binomial: "(1 − 2t)^(−1/2) — a binomial series with a negative fractional index"
  required: ["terms up to t³", "the interval of t for which the series converges"]
  note: "the second half of the question is the half students drop. The validity condition comes from the
         binomial series' own requirement on the magnitude of the term being raised, not from the answer."
```

given:

## Question Five · 4 marks · → `04-maclaurin-theorem` §2, §3

> [q] Use Maclaurin's theorem to expand $\sqrt{x}\ln(x+1)$ as a power series. Hence evaluate, correct to 3
> decimal places, *[4 Marks]*
>
> $$\int_0^{0.5} \sqrt{x}\,\ln(x+1)\,dx$$
>
> ⚠ VERIFY *(strictly, $\sqrt{x}\ln(x+1)$ has no Maclaurin series about 0 — its expansion runs in
> half-integer powers. The intended route is unambiguous, but the wording is loose — erratum P22)*

```yaml
given:
  function: "√x · ln(x+1)"
  expand_to: "a power series"
  then: "integrate term by term from 0 to 0.5, to 3 decimal places"
  method: >
    expand ln(1+x) — which DOES have a Maclaurin series — and multiply through by √x. The result is a
    series in x^(3/2), x^(5/2), x^(7/2), …, which integrates term by term without difficulty.
  why_the_wording_is_loose: >
    a Maclaurin series needs all derivatives at 0, and this product's second derivative does not exist
    there. See erratum P22 — a teaching point rather than a blocker.
  ⚠_previously_examined: >
    THIS IS THE 20 AUGUST 2024 CAT 1's QUESTION 4, VERBATIM — same function, same interval [0, 0.5], same
    "correct to 3 decimal places". Only the mark changed, 5 → 4. See EMT3101-CAT1-2024-08-20.md. That CAT
    file also notes the same METHOD (expand, then integrate the series term by term over a short interval)
    recurs on the 2024 final Q2(a) with e^(2x)sin3x, and on Assignment 1 Q4 with cos2θ/θ^(1/3) — so the
    method has now been set four times with four integrands, two of them non-analytic at the lower limit.
  checked_here: "the integral converges and the term-by-term series converges to it. Verified with sympy,
                 2026-09-04. The value is NOT recorded here — this file is a question bank."
```

## Question Six · 5 marks · → `01-gamma-and-beta-functions` §5

### Q6(a) · 3 marks

> [q] Show clearly that *[3 Marks]*
>
> $$\int_0^1 x^{m-1}(1-x)^{p-1}\,dx = \frac{1}{n}B\!\left(\frac{m}{n},p\right), \qquad n \neq 0.$$
>
> ⚠ VERIFY *(the left-hand side carries no $n$ at all, so as printed the identity is false — the left side
> is simply $B(m,p)$ while the right changes with every $n$. It cannot be "shown". **This is the same defect,
> in the same question, as the 27 Oct 2025 final's Q3(b) — erratum P13.** Logged again here as P21)*

### Q6(b) · 2 marks

> [q] Hence, find the exact value of
>
> $$\int_0^1 x^5(1-x^3)^2\,dx$$

```yaml
given:
  identity_as_printed: "∫₀¹ x^(m−1)(1−x)^(p−1) dx = (1/n)·B(m/n, p),  n ≠ 0"
  identity_corrected: "∫₀¹ x^(m−1)(1−xⁿ)^(p−1) dx = (1/n)·B(m/n, p)"
  see: [erratum P21, erratum P13]
  hence_integral: "∫₀¹ x⁵(1−x³)² dx"
  matching_parameters: {m: 6, n: 3, p: 3}
  why_the_correction_is_certain: >
    the "hence" integral has a CUBE inside the bracket. The printed identity has no slot for it, so the
    question is unreachable as printed — and the corrected identity fits it exactly with m=6, n=3, p=3.
    Confirmed by sympy, 2026-09-04, both routes agreeing. The value is not recorded here.
  teaching_note: >
    prove it by the substitution u = xⁿ. That single substitution is the whole of part (a), and it is what
    makes part (b) a two-line answer instead of an expansion.
```
