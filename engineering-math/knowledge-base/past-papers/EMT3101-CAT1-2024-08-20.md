### The one thing to take from this paper

**Q3 is the end-of-semester paper's Question Four.** Not similar to it — the same differential equation,
$y'' + xy' + 2y = 0$, with the same boundary conditions, $y(0) = 1$ and $y'(0) = 2$. It was set on:

| Paper | Where | Marks | Method allowed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CAT 1**, 20 Aug 2024 | Q3 | 7 | **Leibniz–Maclaurin only** |
| **End of Semester**, 23 Oct 2024 | Q4 | 15 | either Leibniz–Maclaurin **or** Frobenius |
| **End of Semester**, 27 Oct 2025 | Q4(b) | 11 | either Leibniz–Maclaurin **or** Frobenius |

**Three papers over fourteen months, one question, 33 marks between them.** Two papers is a small sample
and three is not much better — but the wording is identical across all three, and the only thing that
changes is the mark and whether Frobenius is offered as an alternative. If any single item in this
subject deserves to be drilled until it is automatic, the evidence points here.

**And it is the one item the knowledge base cannot teach.** Frobenius' method is a documented gap
(`00-index.md` § Gap map: `06` asserts it and the lecturer's margin says *"Homework: show!! (Derive)"*),
and the Leibniz–Maclaurin route from an ODE to a power series is not in `04` or `05` either — those files
build the Maclaurin series and the $n$-th derivative but never point them at a differential equation.
**7 of this paper's 24 marks, 15 of the 2024 final's 90 and 11 of the 2025 final's 90 sit on material he
has not been given.** That is the highest-priority gap in the subject, and the first thing to build if
lecture notes for it ever arrive.

### Two smaller observations

- **Q1 is the same shape as the 2024 final's Q1(c)** — a pdf $f = k\times(\text{variable})$ on an
  interval, normalise, then a moment. Here it is $kt$ on $[0,12]$ and the twist is that $E(T)$ and
  $\mathrm{Var}(T)$ come out equal; there it is $kx$ on $[0,a]$ and the twist is that the paper printed the
  answer wrongly. **Both are file `02` §2–§3 and nothing more.** Q1(d), "sketch $f(t)$ for all $t$", is
  the only *draw-it* instruction on any EMT 3101 paper in this folder.
- **Q4 is the same shape as the 2024 final's Q2(a)** — expand by Maclaurin, then integrate the series
  term by term over a short interval. Here the function is $\sqrt x\,\ln(x+1)$ on $[0, 0.5]$; there it is
  $e^{2x}\sin3x$ on $[0, 0.1]$. It is also the same shape as **Assignment 1's Q4**
  ($\cos2\theta/\theta^{1/3}$ on $[0,1]$). **Three assessments, three different integrands, one method.**
  In two of the three the integrand is singular or non-analytic at the lower limit — see erratum P10.
- **⚠ And Q4 was reissued verbatim.** `EMT3101-TUTORIAL1-2026-08-31.md` Q5 is this question word for word —
  same $\sqrt x\,\ln(x+1)$, same $[0,0.5]$, same "3 decimal places" — set as a tutorial to the 2026
  cohort. Only the mark changed, 5 → 4. **Of the four questions on this CAT, this is the one with direct

## Question One · 7 marks · → `02-crv-and-beta-distribution` §2, §3, §5.2

> [q] The lengths of telephone conversations, in minutes, by sales reps of a certain company are modelled
> by the continuous random variable $T$. The probability density function of $T$ is denoted by $f(t)$, and
> is given by
>
> $$f(t) = \begin{cases} kt & 0 \le t \le 12 \\ 0 & \text{otherwise}\end{cases}$$
>
> **(a)** Show that $k = \frac{1}{72}$ &nbsp;&nbsp; **(b)** Determine $P(T > 5)$ &nbsp;&nbsp;
> **(c)** Show by calculation that $E(T) = Var(T)$
> **(d)** Sketch $f(t)$ for all $t$. *(7 Marks)*

⚠ VERIFY *(all four sub-parts share one undivided [7 Marks] — erratum **P11**; "$Var(T)$" is set in
italic maths and reads as a product — erratum **P12**)*

  pdf: {expression: "k t", interval: "0 ≤ t ≤ 12", elsewhere: 0}
  units_of_T: "minutes"
  asked_for: ["show k = 1/72", "P(T > 5)", "show E(T) = Var(T)", "sketch f(t) for all t"]
data_checked_here:                 # MINE — sympy, 2026-09-03. The paper's own claims are SOUND
  k_from_normalisation: "1/72 ✓ — matches the value the paper asks him to show"
  E_T: 8
  E_T2: 72
  Var_T: "72 - 64 = 8 ✓ — E(T) and Var(T) are both 8, so the paper's claim in (c) is true"
  P_T_gt_5: "119/144 = 0.826389"
  sketch_note: "MINE — f is a straight line from (0,0) to (12, 1/6) and zero elsewhere, so the sketch must

## Question Two · 5 marks · → `01-gamma-and-beta-functions` §2, §5.4

> [q] The Gamma function is defined as $\;\Gamma(x) = \int_0^{\infty}e^{-t}t^{x-1}\,dt, \qquad x \ne 0, -1, -2, -3, \cdots$ *(5 Marks)*
>
> **(i)** Express the integral $\;I(s) = \int_0^{\infty}e^{-p^{s}}\,dp, \quad s > 0,\;$ in terms of the
> Gamma functions.
> **(ii)** Deduce that $\;\lim_{s\to\infty}\left[I(s)\right] = 1$.

⚠ VERIFY *(the stated domain is the domain of the analytically continued $\Gamma$, not of the integral
printed beside it — erratum **P9** ★. The [5 Marks] is also printed on this stem line rather than against
the parts it pays for — erratum **P11**)*

```yaml
given:
  gamma_definition_as_printed: "Γ(x) = ∫₀^∞ e^(-t) t^(x-1) dt,  x ≠ 0, -1, -2, -3, ⋯"
  integral: "I(s) = ∫₀^∞ e^(-p^s) dp,  s > 0"
  asked_for: ["I(s) in terms of Gamma functions", "deduce lim(s→∞) I(s) = 1"]
data_checked_here:                 # MINE — sympy, 2026-09-03
  I_of_s: "Γ((s+1)/s) = Γ(1 + 1/s)"
  limit: "as s → ∞, 1/s → 0 and Γ(1 + 1/s) → Γ(1) = 1 ✓ — the paper's stated result is right"
  domain_problem: "the printed integral converges only for x > 0. At x = -1/2 — which the printed condition
                   ALLOWS — the integrand behaves like t^(-3/2) near 0 and the integral diverges. See P9."
```

## Question Four · 5 marks · → `04-maclaurin-theorem` §2, §3

> [q] Use Maclaurin's theorem to expand $\;\sqrt{x}\ln(x+1)\;$ as a power series. Hence evaluate, correct
> to 3 decimal places, *(5 Marks)*
>
> $$\int_0^{0.5}\sqrt{x}\ln(x+1)\,dx$$

⚠ VERIFY *($\sqrt x\,\ln(x+1)$ has no Maclaurin series — it is not differentiable at the origin. What is
meant is: expand $\ln(1+x)$, then multiply by $\sqrt x$ — erratum **P10**)*

```yaml
given:
  integrand: "√x · ln(x+1)"
  limits: {lower: 0, upper: 0.5}
  precision: "3 decimal places"
  asked_for: ["a power series for √x ln(x+1)", "hence the value of the integral"]
derived_not_printed:               # MINE — the structure of the question, not its answer
  why_the_instruction_is_loose: "a Maclaurin series requires f, f', f'' … to exist at x = 0. Here
                                 f'(x) = ln(1+x)/(2√x) + √x/(1+x) → -∞ as x → 0⁺, so no Maclaurin series
                                 of the PRODUCT exists. Expanding ln(1+x) = x - x²/2 + x³/3 - ⋯ and
                                 multiplying by √x gives x^(3/2) - x^(5/2)/2 + x^(7/2)/3 - ⋯, which is a
                                 Puiseux (fractional-power) series, not a Maclaurin series. It integrates
                                 term by term perfectly well — the marks are safe, the label is not."
  house_pattern: "the same slip appears in Assignment 1 Q4, where θ^(-1/3) makes the integrand singular at
                  θ = 0 and the question does not mention it. Saying so explicitly is a free mark — the
                  past-papers index makes the same point."
```
