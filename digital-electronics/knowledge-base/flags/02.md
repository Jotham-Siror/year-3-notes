### V02-1 · CH2 slides 3 and 4

**Prints:** slide 3 — "SSI *(small-scale integrated)* - fewer than 10 gates … LSI - 100 to thousands
of gates … VLSI - thousands to 100's of millions of gates". Slide 4, headed **"Number of transistors
(BJT or MOSFET)"** — "Small Scale Integration (SSI) - 0 to 10 … Large Scale Integration (LSI) - 100
to 1,000 … Very Large Scale Integration (VLSI) - 1,000 to 10,000 or thousands gates".
**Should be:** one consistent set of boundaries with one consistent unit. Teach slide 3's list as
*gates per chip*, and take from slide 4 only the two tiers slide 3 lacks — SLSI, 10 000 to 100 000
transistors, and ULSI, more than $10^6$ transistors.
**Why:** the two slides give different upper limits for the same term (LSI: "thousands" vs "1,000";
VLSI: "100's of millions" vs "10,000"), and item 4 of slide 4 says "1,000 to 10,000 **or thousands
gates**" — mixing both units inside a single entry under a heading that says transistors.
**Class:** substantive — a definition question would be marked differently depending on which slide
the student memorised.

### V02-2 · CH2 slide 8

**Prints:** "$V_L$ – The nominal voltage corresponding to a low-logic state **at the input** of a
logic gate for $v_i = V_H$"
**Should be:** "…at the **output** of a logic gate for $v_i = V_H$"
**Why:** $V_L$ and $V_H$ are the two output levels; the very next bullet on the same slide defines
$V_H$ as "at the output of a logic gate for $v_i = V_L$", and the figure beside it plots both on the
$v_O$ axis. The input-side quantities on that slide are $V_{IL}$ and $V_{IH}$, defined separately.
**Class:** substantive — it makes $V_L$ an input specification, which collides with $V_{IL}$ and
breaks $NM_L = V_{IL} - V_{OL}$ on the next slide.

### V02-3 · CH2 slide 10

**Prints:** "The high-to-low prop delay, $\tau_{PHL}$, *delay time in going from logical 0 to
logical 1 state (LOW to HIGH)* and the low-to-high prop delay, $\tau_{PLH}$, *delay time in going
from logical 1 to logical 0 state (HIGH to LOW)*"
**Should be:** the two italicised glosses swapped — $\tau_{PHL}$ is the delay on the output
transition **HIGH to LOW**; $\tau_{PLH}$ is the delay on the output transition **LOW to HIGH**.
**Why:** the subscripts already say so, and the waveform on the same slide (repeated on slide 14)
marks $\tau_{PHL}$ on the edge where $v_O$ falls from $V_H$ to $V_L$ and $\tau_{PLH}$ where $v_O$
rises. The printed words contradict the printed figure.
**Class:** substantive — reading a timing diagram with the two swapped gives the wrong delay for
every edge.

### V02-4 · CH2 slide 17

**Prints:** question — "A certain gate draws **2 mA** when its output is HIGH and **3.6 mA** when
its output is LOW"; solution —
$I_{CC} = \dfrac{2\ \mu A + 3.6\ \mu A}{2} = 2.8\ \mu A$ and $P_D = V_{CC}I_{CC} = 5v \times
2.8\ \mu A = 14\ \mu W$
**Should be:** $I_{CC} = 2.8\ \text{mA}$ and $P_D = 5\ \text{V} \times 2.8\ \text{mA} =
14\ \text{mW}$
**Why:** the data are given in milliamps and silently become microamps in the working — a factor of
$10^3$. Check: $(2 + 3.6)/2 = 2.8$ and $5 \times 2.8 = 14$, so only the prefix is wrong, in both
lines. 14 µW is also implausible for a bipolar gate on a 5 V rail.
**Class:** substantive — the numerical answer is out by three orders of magnitude.

### V02-5 · CH2 slide 36

**Prints:** the left-hand circuit, headed "P-channel Enhancement", drawn with $V_{DD}$ at the top
through a resistor to a node labelled DRAIN and $V_{out}$, the device below it, and the terminal
labelled SOURCE tied to ground; caption "To Turn On, Gate Must Be Lower Than Source".
**Should be:** for a p-channel enhancement stage the **source goes to $V_{DD}$** and the drain to
the load resistor down to ground, with $V_{out}$ taken at the drain. The right-hand (n-channel)
circuit as drawn is correct.
**Why:** with the source at 0 V, "gate lower than source" demands a negative gate voltage, which a
0 V to +5 V logic system cannot produce — the device could never turn on. The deck connects the
p-channel device correctly two slides later, in the CMOS inverter of slide 38, where its source sits
at $V_{DD}$.
**Class:** substantive — the circuit as drawn does not function.

### C02-5 · CH2 slide 29

**Prints:** in panel (a), input HIGH, the collector of $Q_2$ is annotated "$\cong 0.7$ V".
**Should be:** $\approx 0.9$ V.
**Why:** with $Q_2$ saturated, that node sits at $V_{BE3} + V_{CE(\text{sat})2} = 0.7 + 0.2 = 0.9$ V.
The argument the annotation supports — that $Q_4$ stays off, needing
$V_{\text{out}} + V_{D2} + V_{BE4} = 0.2 + 0.7 + 0.7 = 1.6$ V — is unaffected either way.
**Class:** cosmetic — the conclusion is unchanged, but the printed node voltage is 0.2 V low.

## Resolved after a screenshot

*Not counted in `verification_flags` — this was a reading difficulty, not a defect.*

- **CH2 slide 24 — the mark on each base lead is a diode.** It was first recorded as an
  unidentified solid arrowhead and omitted from Fig. 2-5. A higher-magnification screenshot,
  supplied 19 Aug 2026, shows the triangle followed by **its own cathode bar** before the lead
  continues into the transistor: a diode in series with the base, anode at the summing node,
  cathode at the base. Fig. 2-5 now draws it, labelled $D$.

  **Why it is there.** $R_1$ returns the base to a negative rail $V-$. With the inputs LOW the base
  would otherwise be driven below the emitter, and emitter-base reverse breakdown is only about
  5 to 7 V; the series diode blocks the reverse current and stands off the reverse voltage.

  **Corroborated on the slide's own page**, not imported from outside: circuit (iii) runs from
  $+V_{CC}$ alone, has no negative rail, and carries **no** diode. The deck never states the
  reasoning in words, so the explanation in the topic file is tagged `[added]`.

  With this resolved, **no figure in the 348 slides is unread.**
