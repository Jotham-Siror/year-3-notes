# Verification flags — 01 Introduction and Recap (CH1, slides 1–32)

Four flags: one substantive, three cosmetic.

---

### V01-1 · CH1 slide 7

**Prints:** under the heading "Types of Systems", the bullet list reads

> - With no state present
>   - Combinational logic system
>   - Output = Function (Input)
> - With state present
>   - State updated at discrete times (e.g. once per clock tick)
> - Synchronous sequential system
>   - State updated at any time
> - Asynchronous sequential system

**Should be:**

> - With no state present → **combinational logic system**; Output = Function(Input)
> - With state present, state updated at discrete times (e.g. once per clock tick) →
>   **synchronous sequential system**
> - With state present, state updated at any time → **asynchronous sequential system**

**Why:** as laid out on the slide, the descriptor "State updated at any time" is the sub-bullet of
"Synchronous sequential system", and "Asynchronous sequential system" is left with no descriptor at
all. Read literally the slide defines *synchronous* as updating at any time, which is the definition
of *asynchronous*. The stranded sub-bullet "State updated at discrete times" sits under the parent
"With state present" with no system name attached to it. The indentation has slipped by one level.

**Class:** substantive — it inverts a definition, and it is the definition the whole of CH5 and the
ripple/synchronous counter distinction on slides 17–20 rest on. A student reproducing slide 7 in an
exam would state the classification backwards.

### C01-1 · CH1 slide 14

**Prints:** three sub-bullets naming signals:

> - Data input, In, is called a *serial input* or the *shift right input*.
> - Data output, Out, is often called the *serial output*.
> - The vector (A, B, C, Out) is called the *parallel output*.

**Should be:** the same statements, but expressed in the labels the accompanying figure actually
carries — $SI$ for "In", $SO$ for "Out", and the four stage outputs left to right for
$(A, B, C, \text{Out})$.

**Why:** the bullets and the figure come from different sources. The figure on slide 14 (reused
unchanged on slides 15 and 16) labels only $SI$, $SO$, $D$ and $C$; the names In, Out, A, B and C
appear nowhere on it. A reader cannot resolve "the vector (A, B, C, Out)" against the picture. The
mapping is $\text{In} = SI$, $\text{Out} = SO$, and $A$, $B$, $C$ are the outputs of the first three
stages counting from the serial input end.

**Class:** cosmetic — no statement is wrong, but the cross-reference between text and figure does not
resolve. Fig. 1-9 in the topic file carries the stage names so the bullet can be read against the
drawing; the added labelling is marked as added on the figure itself.

### C01-2 · CH1 slide 16

**Prints:** two things in the solution to the shift-register example:

1. "Since we have to store 1100 in the register, bits will be entered **LSB**"
2. Remark column: row 1 "They are entered from the right", row 3 "As they are shifted from left"

**Should be:**

1. "… bits will be entered **LSB first**"
2. Row 1 should make clear it is the *data word* that is read from its right-hand end; row 3 that it
   is the *register* that is filled from its left-hand end. For example: "the least significant bit
   of 1100 is entered first" and "each bit enters at the leftmost flip-flop and shifts right".

**Why:** (1) the sentence is truncated mid-phrase and, as printed, does not say in what order. (2)
Read side by side, "entered from the right" and "shifted from left" appear to contradict each other;
they refer to two different objects. Feeding the bits in the other order gives $0011$, not $1100$, so
a reader who resolves the ambiguity the wrong way gets the reversed nibble.

The numbers themselves are correct: the printed table $0000, 0000, 0000, 1000, 1100$ was rederived
by simulating $A \leftarrow SI,\ B \leftarrow A,\ C \leftarrow B,\ \text{Out} \leftarrow C$ from a
cleared register with serial input $0, 0, 1, 1$, and reproduces row for row.

**Class:** cosmetic — wording only; the state table and the highlighted answer $1000$ after three
pulses are both right.

### C01-3 · CH1 slides 10 and 27

**Prints:**

- Slide 10 — 74AC00, Advanced CMOS 2-input NAND gate, at 30 pF load: $t_{PHL}$ min 1.5, typ 4.5,
  max 6.5 ns; $t_{PLH}$ min 1.5, typ 6.0, max 8.0 ns.
- Slide 27 — "Older families slow (4000 series about 60ns), but new ones (**74AC**) much faster
  (**3ns**)."

**Should be:** the two slides should quote the same family under the same conditions, or slide 27
should state the conditions under which 3 ns applies.

**Why:** the same logic family is given a typical propagation delay of 4.5–6.0 ns on one slide and
about 3 ns on another, within one deck. Propagation delay is a strong function of supply voltage and
load capacitance, and only slide 10 states its load (30 pF), so the figures are not necessarily
inconsistent physically — but as printed there is no way for a reader to reconcile them. The slide 10
table, which states its conditions, is the one to quote.

**Class:** cosmetic — an internal inconsistency between two slides rather than an arithmetic error.
Flagged because a numerical answer would differ depending on which slide the figure is taken from.

## For a human to look at

- **The deck footer.** Every slide carries a social handle in the footer instead of a name. It has
  been treated as a contact detail under the repository's rule 2 and is not reproduced anywhere in
  the topic file, the figures or this log. No personal name appears on any of the 32 slides.
- **Third-party imagery.** Slides 2, 4, 6, 8, 9, 17, 26 and 30 are photographs, textbook scans or
  tool screenshots. All are captioned rather than reproduced. Slide 4 in particular is a widely
  circulated microprocessor-trend chart and should not be reproduced under any circumstances.
- **Slide 8, minor.** The printed equation for $\text{out}6$ includes the term
  $\text{in}3 \cdot \text{in}2 \cdot \overline{\text{in}1} \cdot \overline{\text{in}0}$, which covers
  only input 1100 — a don't-care for a decimal seven-segment decoder. The equation is therefore
  correct over every specified row but not minimal. Not flagged, because the montage is illustrative
  third-party material and the redundancy changes no stated result.
