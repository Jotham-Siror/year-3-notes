# Verification flags — 06 — Digital-to-Analogue Conversion

Source: `CH4`, slides 30–49 of 49. Nine substantive defects, six cosmetic.

Every equation on these slides was checked for dimensional and algebraic consistency, symbol slips,
mislabelled results and self-reference; every numerical claim was recomputed in Python, and the
R/2R ladder of slides 44–47 was additionally re-solved by nodal analysis with the printed component
values rather than trusted to the printed formula.

---

### V06-1 · CH4 slide 34

**Prints:** "$2^{N} - 1$ = no of states, $N$ − no of bits"
**Should be:** $2^{N}$ is the number of states; $2^{N}-1$ is the number of **steps** between them.
**Why:** an $N$-bit word has $2^N$ distinct codes, so $2^N$ output levels, with $2^N-1$ gaps between
the lowest and highest. Slide 48 states the intended reading itself — resolution is "the reciprocal
of the number of **steps** in the output".
**Class:** substantive — the formula above the gloss is correct, but the gloss teaches a wrong fact
about state counts that carries into every later question.

---

### V06-2 · CH4 slide 35

**Prints:** $V_{res} = \dfrac{5}{1023} = 8.888 \times 10^{-3}\ V$
**Should be:** $4.888 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{V} = 4.888\ \text{mV}$
**Why:** $5/1023 = 0.0048876$. Reverse check: $8.888\ \text{mV} \times 1023 = 9.09$ V, not the 5 V
full scale the question states.
**Class:** substantive — a leading-digit slip in the only number the example produces.

---

### V06-3 · CH4 slide 36

**Prints:** $40 \times 10^{-6}A < \dfrac{10\times10^{-3}A}{2^{N}-1}$, then
$2^{N} < \dfrac{10\times10^{-3}}{40\times10^{-6}} + 1$, then $2^{N} < 251$, then
$N = \dfrac{\log_{10}251}{\log_{10}2} = 7.97 \approx 8\ bits$
**Should be:** $\dfrac{10\times10^{-3}}{2^{N}-1} < 40\times10^{-6} \Rightarrow 2^{N} > 251
\Rightarrow N > 7.97 \Rightarrow N = 8$ bits.
**Why:** the requirement is that the step be *smaller* than 40 µA, which puts the resolution on the
small side of the inequality, not the large. As printed the chain gives $N \le 7$, contradicting the
answer stated on the same line. Direct check: at $N=7$ the step is $10\ \text{mA}/127 = 78.7\ \mu$A
(fails); at $N=8$ it is $10\ \text{mA}/255 = 39.2\ \mu$A (passes).
**Class:** substantive — the final answer is right, the working that reaches it is not, and a student
reproducing the printed chain in an exam answers 7.

### V06-4 · CH4 slide 37

**Prints:** for a DAC the question states "uses a BCD input code":
$2^{N}-1 = 2^{12}-1 = 4095$; $\text{Resolution} = 9.99/4095 = 0.00244$;
$V_{out} = 695 \times 0.00244 = 1.6978$
**Should be:** a 12-bit BCD word is three decades, each stopping at $1001_2 = 9$, so it counts
$000$–$999$: **999** steps, not 4095. Step size $= 9.99/999 = 0.01\ \text{V} = 10$ mV; percentage
resolution $= (1/999)\times100\,\% = 0.1\,\%$; $V_{out} = 695 \times 0.01 = 6.95$ V.
**Why:** the full-scale figure in the question is the confirmation — $9.99\ \text{V}$ is exactly
$999 \times 10\ \text{mV}$, a number that has no meaning on the binary route. The BCD reading of the
input code, $0110\,1001\,0101 = 695$, is the slide's own.
**Class:** substantive — the binary formula has been applied to a BCD-coded converter, and all three
answers of the question change.

### V06-5 · CH4 slide 37

**Prints:** $Step\ size = 2^{N}-1 = 2^{12}\text{-}1 = 4095$
**Should be:** $\text{number of steps} = 999$ (BCD); the step **size** is a voltage,
$9.99\ \text{V}/999 = 10$ mV.
**Why:** dimensional check — a step size has the units of the output, and $4095$ is a pure count.
The line that follows then divides by that same count to obtain the step, so the label on this line
contradicts the line beneath it.
**Class:** substantive — a mislabelled result; the quantity computed is not the quantity named.

---

### V06-6 · CH4 slide 37

**Prints:** $\text{Resolution} = \dfrac{9.99}{4095} = 0.00244 = 0.244\%$
**Should be:** $0.00244$ is a step in **volts** and cannot be restated as a percentage. The
percentage resolution is $1/(2^N-1) \times 100\,\%$ — that is $0.0244\,\%$ on the slide's own binary
route, and $0.1\,\%$ on the corrected BCD route.
**Why:** the percentage form is the step divided by full scale, $0.00244/9.99 = 2.44\times10^{-4}$,
not the step multiplied by 100. Slide 48 performs exactly this conversion correctly for the 8-bit
case ($1/255 = 0.39\,\%$), so the two slides disagree.
**Class:** substantive — off by a factor of ten even before the BCD correction of V06-4.

### V06-7 · CH4 slide 37

**Prints:** $V_{out} = 695 \times 0.00244 = 1.6978$
**Should be:** $695 \times 0.00244 = 1.6958$; with the unrounded step $9.99/4095$ it is $1.6955$; the
corrected BCD answer is $6.95$ V.
**Why:** straight multiplication. No unit is printed on the result either.
**Class:** substantive — an arithmetic slip independent of V06-4, so the line is wrong on its own
terms as well.

---

### V06-8 · CH4 slide 38

**Prints:** $Error = \dfrac{2\ mA \times 0.19}{100} = 3.8\ \mu A$
**Should be:** $E = 0.19 \times 2\ \text{mA} = 0.38\ \text{mA} = 380\ \mu$A
**Why:** $0.19$ already **is** $19/100$; dividing by 100 again applies the percentage twice. The
line immediately above, $19\% = \dfrac{E}{2\ \text{mA}}\times100\%$, rearranges to
$E = 19 \times 2\ \text{mA}/100$ — with 19, not 0.19, over the 100.
**Class:** substantive — the printed answer is 100 times too small, and a full-scale error of a few
microamps on a 2 mA converter is not a $\pm19\,\%$ specification.

### V06-9 · CH4 slides 44 and 47

**Prints:** $V_{out} = -\dfrac{V_S}{2^{\,n-i}}$ where "$n$ = number of bits, $i$ = bit number"
(slide 44), applied on slide 47 to give $V_{out}(D_0) = -5/2^{4-0} = -0.3125$ V,
$V_{out}(D_1) = -5/2^{4-1} = -0.625$ V, $V_{out}(D_3) = -5/2^{4-3} = -2.5$ V and, by superposition,
$V_{out} = -3.4375$ V for the input 1011.
**Should be:** $V_{out}(D_i) = -\dfrac{V_S}{2^{\,n-1-i}}$ for inputs labelled $D_0 \ldots D_{n-1}$,
giving $-0.625$, $-1.25$, $-2.5$ and $-5$ V for $D_0$ to $D_3$, and
$V_{out} = -5 - 1.25 - 0.625 = -6.875$ V for the input 1011. Equivalently
$V_{out} = -V_S D / 2^{\,n-1}$ with $D$ the decimal value of the code.
**Why:** two independent checks. (1) The deck contradicts itself: slides 45(a) and 46 analyse the
same network from the circuit and obtain $-5$ V for $D_3$ alone, where the printed formula gives
$-2.5$ V. (2) A nodal solution of the ladder with the slide-47 values ($R = 25$ kΩ, $2R = 50$ kΩ,
$R_f = 50$ kΩ, $V_S = 5$ V) returns $-6.875$ V for 1011 and the single-bit weights $-0.625$,
$-1.25$, $-2.5$, $-5$ V — exactly a factor of two above every value the printed formula produces.
The printed exponent would be correct only if the bits were numbered $1$ to $n$; the deck numbers
them $0$ to $n-1$ in its own figures.
**Class:** substantive — the formula is the deck's only stated result for the R/2R ladder, it is
carried into the worked example, and it halves every answer.

### C06-3 · CH4 slides 44 and 47

**Prints:** "50 kW", "25 kW", "$R_f$ = 50 kW"
**Should be:** $50\ \text{k}\Omega$, $25\ \text{k}\Omega$, $R_f = 50\ \text{k}\Omega$
**Why:** the ohm sign has been substituted by a W — a Symbol-font mapping lost in the file. It is in
the rendered page as well as the text layer, so it is in the slide itself, not an artefact of
extraction. Slides 40, 41 and 43 print the same values with a correct Ω.
**Class:** cosmetic — but worth knowing, because "50 kW" also reads as a power.

### C06-4 · CH4 slide 43

**Prints:** $I_{out} = -\left(\dfrac{3.0\ V}{120\ k\Omega} + 0\ V + \dfrac{3.0\ V}{30\ k\Omega} + \dfrac{3.0\ V}{15\ k\Omega}\right) = -0.325\ mA$
**Should be:** the second term is $\dfrac{0\ \text{V}}{60\ \text{k}\Omega} = 0$ mA
**Why:** a voltage inside a sum of currents. The total is unaffected because the term is zero.
**Class:** cosmetic.

### C06-5 · CH4 slide 39

**Prints:** $V_{out} = I_f R_f$
**Should be:** $V_{out} = -I_f R_f$
**Why:** the amplifier is in the inverting configuration with the summing node at virtual ground.
Slide 41 states the output "is negative with respect to virtual ground" and slide 42 tabulates
sixteen negative values, so the minus is dropped only here.
**Class:** cosmetic — no number depends on it, but the sign is the one thing students lose marks on.
