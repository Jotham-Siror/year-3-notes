## What this paper adds

**This is the paper the CAT 1 file said had to exist.** `BEE3102-CAT1-2024-08-06.md` observed that
158 slides of Chapter 5 and Chapter 6 material — the largest block in the unit — scored **zero** on
CAT 1, and predicted that CAT 2 and the final examination would have to go there. They did. **All 29
printed marks on this paper come out of Chapter 5**, and nothing else on the paper touches CH1–CH4
or CH6 at all.

| Source | Marks | Share |
|---|---|---|
| **CH5 · FSM analysis and design (`07`, `08`)** | **14** | 48 % |
| **CH5 · state reduction (`09`)** | **15** | 52 % |
| CH1, CH2, CH3, CH4, CH6 | **0** | 0 % |

Three things follow.

- **The two 2024 papers are disjoint.** CAT 1 was 50 % DAC and 27 % logic families and touched no
  FSM at all; CAT 2 is 100 % FSM and touches no converter at all. Taken together the pair covers
  `02`, `04`, `06`, `07`, `08`, `09` and leaves `01`, `03`, `05` and `10` unexamined. Two papers is
  not a rule, but the *complementarity* is a strong hint about how this unit is assessed.
- **Question 1's machine is the deck's own worked case.** Figure 1 is a Moore machine over four
  states with output 1 in the last state only; `09-state-reduction-and-assignment.md` §9.10.2 works
  a **Case II — overlapping 101 detector** and `08-sequential-circuit-design.md` §8.6/§8.8 build the
  Mealy and Moore 101 detectors from scratch. Read those three sections before this question.
- **Question 3 needs `09` §9.5 and §9.4 together.** (a) and (b) are the implication-table procedure;
  (c) is the *formal* equivalence theorem of §9.4 — outputs equal for every input **and** next
  states equivalent. A student who only knows the chart procedure cannot answer (c).

**No coverage gap.** Every mark on this paper lands on taught, verified material. That is unusual —
CAT 1 2024 put 3 of its 30 marks outside the decks entirely.

**One warning.** `08` §8.3 is where the overlapping / non-overlapping distinction is defined, and the
index records **V08-1** against ·CH5 slide 53 — a Moore specification copy-pasted from the Mealy one,
"the single most commonly lost mark in the sequence-detector topic". Question 1(i)–(iii) sits exactly
on that distinction. Teach the corrected form.

## Question 2 · 8 marks · → `09-state-reduction-and-assignment` §9.3, §9.5

> [q] A sequential circuit has a single input (X) and a single output (Z). The circuit examines each
> disjoint block of four inputs and determines whether the block is a valid BCD representation of a
> decimal digit; if not, Z = 1. State S0 is the initial state, and the circuit enters state S0 after
> the fourth input. The BCD digits are received most significant bit first. A state table, table 1,
> was constructed as a Mealy table using a sufficient number of states to remember the last three
> inputs with the output produced when the fourth input bit of a block is received. Eliminate the
> redundant states for this sequence detector and draw the resulting state table and diagram. Show
> all the steps. *(8 Marks)*
