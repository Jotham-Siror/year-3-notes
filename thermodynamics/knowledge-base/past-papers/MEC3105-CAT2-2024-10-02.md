### Q3A · 1 mark

> [q] Describe the Rankine cycle and its significance in power generation. *1 Mark*

### Q3B · 4 marks · ⚠ **cannot be answered as printed**

> [q] A Rankine cycle operates with superheated steam at 500°C and a condenser temperature of 45°C. Assuming
> no losses, determine the amount of work done by the turbine if the mass flow rate of steam is 2 kg/s. Use
> steam tables for enthalpy values. *4Marks*
> ⚠ VERIFY *(no turbine inlet PRESSURE is given, so "superheated steam at 500 °C" fixes no state and no
> enthalpy can be read from any table — the question is unanswerable as printed. **erratum P12**)*

```yaml
given:
  cycle: "Rankine, no losses (i.e. isentropic turbine)"
  turbine_inlet: {temperature: {value: 500, unit: "°C"}, pressure: "❌ NOT GIVEN — see erratum P12"}
  condenser_temperature: {value: 45, unit: "°C"}
  mass_flow_rate: {value: 2, unit: "kg/s"}
  asked_for: "the work done by the turbine"
  why_it_fails: >
    a superheated state needs TWO independent properties. Temperature alone puts the state anywhere along the
    500 °C isotherm, and h at 500 °C ranges over hundreds of kJ/kg across the pressures a Rankine plant might
    use. The condenser temperature fixes only the exit pressure.
  how_to_handle: >
    do not guess a pressure. If setting it as practice, SUPPLY one explicitly, say that you are supplying it
    and that the paper did not, and pick something ordinary for a steam plant. Then the method — isentropic
    expansion to the condenser pressure, dryness fraction at exit, w = h1 − h2, times ṁ — is exactly the
    method Q4(B) needs, so the two questions can be taught together.
  coverage: "❌ the Rankine cycle is named in TC s13 and tabulated in HE s12 and analysed in neither"
```
