### Q1B · → `02-first-law` §2.4 · ⚠ **the V8 sign-convention trap**

> [q] A gas is contained in a cylinder with a movable piston. The system is initially at a state where the
> internal energy of the gas is 500 J. The gas is then heated, and 200 J of heat is added to the system.
> During this process, the gas expands and does 150 J of work on the piston. Using the First Law of
> Thermodynamics, calculate the final internal energy of the gas after the heating and expansion process.

```yaml
given:
  initial_internal_energy: {value: 500, unit: J}
  heat_added_to_system: {value: 200, unit: J}
  work_done_by_gas_on_piston: {value: 150, unit: J}
  asked_for: "the final internal energy"
kb_flag: >
  ⚠ THIS IS EXACTLY THE CASE V8 IS ABOUT. FL states two contradictory sign conventions for W and its own
  worked example mixes them, giving an answer wrong by a factor of five. See _nomenclature.md clash 1 and
  _verification-log.md V8. The question is unambiguous — it says the gas DOES work ON the piston, i.e. work
  leaves the system — so ΔU = Q − W is the form that matches the wording. A student revising from FL's
  worked example will apply the other sign and get an answer 300 J out.
teaching_value: >
  do not just mark this right or wrong. Work it both ways in front of him, show that only one of the two
  answers is consistent with "the gas expands", and then show him the slide that would have misled him.
  This single question justifies the whole nomenclature clash list.
```
